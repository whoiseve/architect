01 · Abstract

What
is AAES?

The Adaptive Acoustic Environment System is a closed-loop acoustic control system designed to transform studio acoustics from a static constraint into a dynamic, measurable, and recallable parameter within the production workflow.

By combining real-time acoustic measurement, intelligent control logic, and motorized physical acoustic structures, AAES enables engineers to define, reproduce, and manipulate the acoustic behavior of a recording space with a level of precision and repeatability previously limited to digital environments.

AAES bridges the gap between analog acoustic spaces and digital production tools by allowing full session recall not only of signal chains and routing, but of the physical acoustic environment itself — turning the room into a controllable, repeatable, and shareable production parameter.

Conceptualized and developed by the founder of User Experience Media Group, Geordie Allen, AAES represents a fundamental rethinking of how acoustic spaces are treated within modern hybrid production workflows.

The
Problem

Modern recording studios operate within fundamentally static acoustic environments. Four core limitations restrict creative and operational potential.

01

Static Acoustics

Rooms are designed with a fixed acoustic profile, forcing engineers to adapt recording techniques to the space rather than shaping the space to the source material. The room is a constraint, not a tool.

02

Inconsistent Recall

While DAWs allow full recall of digital sessions, the acoustic environment cannot be restored with precision. Changes in setup, mic positioning, and environmental conditions introduce variability between sessions.

03

Reliance on Human Perception

Acoustic decisions are often made subjectively, based on experience and intuition rather than continuous, measurable data. This limits repeatability and objective optimization across sessions and engineers.

04

Variability Between Sessions

Differences in room setup, mic placement, and acoustic conditions lead to inconsistencies across takes, sessions, and projects — introducing inefficiency and reducing the quality ceiling for long-form work.

5.1 · Sensing Layer
Acoustic Telemetry

Continuously monitoring the room's acoustic behavior through a distributed measurement microphone network at strategic positions throughout the space.

  • RT60 / decay time by frequency band
  • Frequency response · Early reflection behavior
  • Room modes / low-frequency buildup
  • SPL · Noise floor · Impulse response
  • Live room center · Drum · Vocal · Corners
  • Mix position · Reverb chamber pickups
5.2 · Analysis Engine
Real-Time Processing

The analysis engine processes live acoustic telemetry and extracts meaningful engineering parameters for display and decision-making.

  • Multi-band RT60 calculation
  • Impulse response analysis
  • Early vs late reflection separation
  • Modal analysis (low-frequency behavior)
  • Energy decay curve modeling
  • Comparison against target acoustic profiles
5.3 · Control Logic
Decision Layer

Translates acoustic goals into physical system adjustments, supporting manual control, intelligent suggestions, and target-based optimization.

  • Manual control mapping
  • Assisted suggestions from measurement data
  • Target-based optimization
  • Safety and mechanical constraint handling
5.4 · Actuation
Physical Systems

Motorized acoustic structures that physically reshape the room's behavior. Multi-point ceiling clouds, deployable GOBO panels, and variable bass traps.

  • Motorized ceiling cloud · Multi-point Z-axis control
  • Independent tilt and geometry · Encoder position tracking
  • QRD · Skyline · Hybrid diffusion panels
  • Absorption panels · Adjustable ratios
  • Motorized corner bass traps
  • Adjustable cavity depth
5.5 · Recall System
Full State Memory

Complete restoration of the acoustic environment — physical actuator positions, measurement snapshots, and system configuration states all stored per session.

  • Physical positions of all actuators
  • Acoustic measurement snapshots
  • System configuration states
  • DAW session linking
  • Versioning and snapshot workflow
5.6 · Network Layer
Infrastructure

High-performance network backbone supporting real-time data streaming, remote session capability, and facility-wide system communication.

  • 10G–25G fiber backbone
  • Cat6A–Cat8 endpoints
  • Central server and NAS
  • Real-time data streaming
  • PoE for control devices and sensors
  • Dedicated remote session network

07 · Control

Four Control Modes

Manual

Direct engineer control of all actuators. Full hands-on positioning of ceiling clouds, GOBO panels, and bass traps without system intervention.

Assisted

System provides real-time recommendations based on live acoustic measurements. Engineer reviews and accepts or overrides suggestions.

Target

Select a predefined acoustic goal — tight drum room, vocal booth, orchestral space — and the system adjusts the environment to match the target profile.

Recall

Loads a previously saved acoustic state. Restores all physical actuator positions and configurations to recreate an exact prior environment.

08 · Recall

Open a session.
Restore everything.

Acoustic memory,
built into
the workflow.

AAES integrates directly with DAW workflows, allowing acoustic states to be session-linked and restored automatically or on demand. Every parameter of the physical room is stored alongside the digital session.

Resume a project days or weeks later with complete environmental consistency — eliminating variability between takes and revisions.

◈
Passive Recall
DAW references saved acoustic state as metadata. Engineer can review and manually restore the environment.
⊕
Active Recall
DAW triggers AAES to physically reconfigure the room on session load — fully automated restoration of the acoustic environment.

13 · Future Expansion

Where AAES
is going.

AAES is designed as a modular and extensible system. Its architecture supports a range of future capabilities that expand both technical function and its role in modern production.

13.1

AI-Assisted Optimization

Machine learning models trained on accumulated acoustic data — physical configurations, measured responses, and user-selected outcomes — to predict optimal configurations and reduce setup time.

Machine Learning
13.2

Predictive Acoustic Modeling

Shifting from reactive measurement to predictive modeling: pre-visualizing acoustic outcomes before physical movement, enabling simulation-based workflows before committing to adjustments.

Predictive System
13.3

Cross-Room Synchronization

In multi-AAES facilities, matching acoustic profiles across different live rooms, enabling consistent recording environments for multi-room sessions and distributed workflows.

Multi-Room
13.4

Remote Acoustic Matching

Engineers in different locations matching acoustic environments via AAES-enabled systems — remote collaboration with consistent spatial characteristics across facilities and projects.

Remote · Collaboration
13.5

Automated IR Generation

AAES as a persistent acoustic sampling engine — automated capture of IR datasets across defined parameter ranges, building continuously growing proprietary acoustic libraries.

IR · Library
13.7

Broader Production Integration

Integration with lighting and stage automation (DMX), virtual production environments, spatial audio and immersive monitoring systems, and real-time live performance control.

DMX · Spatial Audio
